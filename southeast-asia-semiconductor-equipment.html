Southeast Asia's Semiconductor Growth: What It Means for Equipment Buyers

Southeast Asia is progressing from assembly towards advanced packaging, design and specialty fabs. This article considers what that shift means when selecting equipment and support.

Dymek Editorial Team

Southeast Asia has formed part of the semiconductor supply chain for more than 50 years. Today, the region is moving up the value chain, from basic assembly towards advanced packaging, test, design and specialty manufacturing.

Malaysia: from assembly hub to advanced packaging

Intel opened its first overseas assembly plant in Penang in 1972. Since then, Malaysia has become a global centre for back-end manufacturing. According to a 2024 article published by the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA):

  • Malaysia holds approximately 13% of global semiconductor testing and packaging.
  • The industry accounts for approximately 25% of Malaysia's GDP.
  • The National Semiconductor Strategy begins with RM25 billion in public funding. It aims to attract RM500 billion of investment, train 60,000 chip engineers and develop at least 10 Malaysian companies in design and advanced packaging.

A wider regional shift

Across the region, manufacturers are diversifying their supply chains and investing in new capacity. Singapore has well-established strengths in wafer fabrication and equipment. Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines are expanding their electronics, assembly and test activities. Together, they form a connected manufacturing network.

What this means for equipment buyers

Moving up the value chain changes what manufacturers require from their equipment and suppliers.

1. More advanced processes. Advanced packaging, hybrid bonding, fine-pitch assembly and photonics require tighter process control and more capable metrology.

2. Faster ramps. New lines must achieve yield quickly. Feasibility trials and application support before purchase help to reduce risk.

3. Local service is important. When a tool goes down, waiting for an engineer to fly in results in lost output. Local field service, spare parts and training help to shorten downtime.

4. Multi-site support. Many companies operate plants in several countries. A supplier with offices across the region is able to support all of them consistently.

5. Data and yield. As products become more complex, linking inspection, metrology and test data becomes central to yield learning.
