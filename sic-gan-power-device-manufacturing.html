SiC and GaN Power Device Manufacturing: Key Process, Test and Inspection Challenges

Wide-bandgap power devices are at the heart of EV inverters and fast chargers. Their materials make every process step more demanding. This article outlines where the principal challenges lie.

Silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) can handle higher voltages, temperatures and switching speeds than silicon. This makes them central to EV traction inverters, on-board chargers, fast-charging stations and efficient power supplies. Manufacturing them, however, is more demanding at almost every step.

Why wide-bandgap materials are more demanding

  • SiC is extremely hard and brittle. Its hardness approaches that of diamond, which makes grinding, polishing and dicing slow and wear-intensive.
  • Crystal defects are more significant. Defects in SiC substrates and epitaxy can cause device failure or gradual degradation over time.
  • GaN is often grown on other substrates, such as silicon, which introduces stress, wafer bow and defect-related challenges.
  • Devices operate at high temperatures and voltages, so packages and interconnects are subject to severe thermal and electrical stress.

Key process challenges

Wafer thinning and dicing. Thinner wafers reduce on-resistance, but thin SiC is fragile. Blade dicing of SiC is slow and may cause chipping. Laser-based separation methods can improve edge quality, processing speed and mechanical strength.

Backside contacts. After thinning, SiC devices require a low-resistance ohmic contact on the backside. Selective laser annealing can form these contacts whilst limiting the heat reaching the finished front-side structures.

Thermal and plasma processing. High-temperature steps, together with controlled plasma etching and deposition, form the device structures and passivation.

Thin-film metrology. Film thickness and film properties must be carefully controlled across the wafer.

Test and inspection

Wafer inspection. Automated optical inspection identifies surface defects and process abnormalities at an early stage, before further value is added.

High-voltage, high-current wafer test. Wafer-level probing across a range of controlled temperatures characterises device performance and reliability prior to packaging.

Package inspection and failure analysis. Power modules employ sintered die attach, thick wire bonds and large solder areas. X-ray, CT and scanning acoustic microscopy are used to detect voids and delamination. High-resolution analysis then investigates metallisation and interconnect damage following stress testing.

The wider picture

As the EV and energy markets grow, additional wide-bandgap capacity is being built, including across Asia. Each new line requires process equipment, metrology and test capability proven on SiC and GaN, together with local engineers to support the ramp-up.
